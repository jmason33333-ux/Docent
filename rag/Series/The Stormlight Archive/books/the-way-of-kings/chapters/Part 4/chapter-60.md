## Quick Summary

**Dalinar Kholin:** Dalinar decides not to abdicate his position to his son Adolin, opting instead to face the challenges ahead himself. As a highstorm approaches, Dalinar gathers with Adolin, Renarin, and Navani in his chambers. Navani demonstrates a new fabrial, an early model painrial, to ease an ache in Adolin's hand. Despite advancements in fabrial technology, the group acknowledges that creating new Shardblades and Shardplate remains beyond their reach, speculating that these were gifts from the Heralds.

As the highstorm hits, Dalinar enters another vision. He finds himself conversing with a regal man, whom he later recognizes as a younger Nohadon, author of "The Way of Kings." The Desolation has recently ended, leaving 90% of Nohadon's people dead. Survivors blame Surgebinder Alakavish for initiating a war just before the Desolation. Nohadon muses about abandoning his throne, doubting his ability to lead effectively. Dalinar, whom Nohadon mistakes for his advisor Karm, suggests Nohadon write a book on leadership and philosophy. Nohadon dismisses the idea, emphasizing that action and the sword are necessary for peace, noting, "to be human is to want that which we cannot have." As Nohadon departs, Dalinar repeats this sentiment.

Returning to the present, Dalinar feels he hasn't gained any new insights into validating his visions. Navani interrupts, urging him to repeat what he said. She reveals that Dalinar spoke in Dawnchant, a dead language, and his recent phrase might unlock the key to translating it. Navani asserts that this discovery confirms the authenticity of Dalinar's visions.

## Rowan's "If Asked" Notes

**Q: What is the significance of Navani's painrial fabrial demonstration?**
A: Navani's demonstration highlights advancements in fabrial technology, showcasing their potential for healing. It also underscores the limitations in replicating ancient magics like Shardblades.

**Q: Why does Dalinar decide not to abdicate to Adolin?**
A: Dalinar feels a sense of duty and responsibility to continue leading, believing he must face the challenges himself rather than passing them to Adolin prematurely.

**Q: Who is Nohadon, and why is he significant?**
A: Nohadon is the author of "The Way of Kings," a key philosophical text. In Dalinar's vision, he grapples with leadership challenges after a Desolation, providing insights into effective rule.

**Q: How does Dalinar's vision with Nohadon impact him?**
A: The vision reinforces Dalinar's commitment to leadership and introduces the idea of writing a philosophy on leadership, despite Nohadon's initial dismissal.

**Q: What does speaking in Dawnchant imply about Dalinar's visions?**
A: Navani identifies Dalinar's speech as Dawnchant, suggesting his visions are genuine and may unlock the means to translate ancient texts.

**Q: Why is Alakavish mentioned in the vision?**
A: Alakavish is blamed for starting a war before a Desolation, illustrating the consequences of Surgebinder actions and the chaos they can cause.

**Q: What does "The death is my life, the strength becomes my weakness, the journey has ended" mean?**
A: The epigraph reflects the paradoxes in Dalinar's journey and foreshadows themes of transformation and sacrifice.

**Q: What does the phrase "to be human is to want that which we cannot have" reveal about Nohadon?**
A: Nohadon's statement reflects the inherent conflict between desire and reality, highlighting a central theme in human experience and leadership challenges.
