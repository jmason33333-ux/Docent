## Quick Summary

**Adolin:**
Adolin Kholin spends time at a wineshop in the outer Market with his date Danlan, and friends Jakamav, Inkima, Toral, and Eshava. They discuss the precarious political situation concerning his father, Dalinar Kholin, and the reasons why Sadeas has not openly acted against him. Adolin is reflective about Dalinar's efforts to unify and discipline the Alethi armies, contrasting them with the laxity he observes in other officers. He is slowly coming to appreciate Dalinar's stern approach. Adolin leaves the wineshop to prepare for a duel against Brightlord Resi, pondering the state of the war effort and his father's role in it.

**Dalinar:**
At the dueling grounds, Dalinar Kholin, alongside Sadeas and King Elhokar, watches lesser lighteyes duel in anticipation of Adolin's match. Dalinar recites a passage from "The Way of Kings," reflecting on Nohadon's journey to Urithiru, which Sadeas dismisses derisively. Despite Sadeas's insults, Dalinar remains composed. Elhokar jests about Sadeas replacing Hoid as Wit, and the conversation shifts to serious matters. Dalinar proposes withdrawing the army to stabilize Alethkar and to send envoys to investigate Gavilar's murder, a suggestion Elhokar praises. As Adolin duels and wins easily, Dalinar agrees to Elhokar's proposal for a joint assault with Sadeas's forces as an experiment. Sadeas requests a clerk be sent with a copy of "The Way of Kings."

## Rowan's "If Asked" Notes

**Q: What is the significance of Dalinar's reading from "The Way of Kings"?**
A: Dalinar's reading reflects his philosophical approach to leadership, emphasizing the journey and the unity of the Alethi people. It contrasts sharply with Sadeas's pragmatic and sometimes cynical views.

**Q: Why does Sadeas request a copy of "The Way of Kings"?**
A: Despite mocking Dalinar's ideals, Sadeas requests the book to better understand Dalinar's mindset, possibly for strategic advantage.

**Q: How does Adolin feel about his father's leadership?**
A: Adolin is conflicted but increasingly appreciates Dalinar’s disciplined approach, recognizing its necessity amid the political and military challenges they face.

**Q: What is the joint assault experiment proposed by Elhokar?**
A: Elhokar suggests a cooperative military strategy where Dalinar and Sadeas combine forces, testing the effectiveness of their bridge crews working together.

**Q: How does Elhokar's paranoia manifest in this chapter?**
A: Elhokar speaks of seeing inhuman faces in mirrors and fears plots against him, suggesting deeper psychological issues or potential threats.

**Q: What is the significance of Adolin's duel with Resi?**
A: The duel showcases Adolin's prowess and serves as a metaphor for the larger political and military battles he and his family are engaged in.

**Q: Who is Re-Shephir, mentioned in the epigraph?**
A: Re-Shephir, the Midnight Mother, is one of the Unmade, ancient beings of significant power and darkness, suggesting a malevolent presence.

**Q: Why does Dalinar suggest withdrawing the army to Alethkar?**
A: Dalinar believes that stabilizing their homeland is crucial for the kingdom's long-term strength and proposes investigating the circumstances of Gavilar's assassination.
