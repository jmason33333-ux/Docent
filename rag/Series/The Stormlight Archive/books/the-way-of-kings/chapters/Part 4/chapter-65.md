## Quick Summary

**Dalinar:** Dalinar Kholin and Torol Sadeas stand together watching the Parshendi army, numbering around ten thousand, as they attempt to retrieve a gemheart. Sadeas proposes a joint attack, but Dalinar, adhering to the Codes which dictate that a leader should not ask of his men what he wouldn't do himself, hesitates because the bridgemen will likely suffer heavy casualties. He decides to follow Sadeas's army after a bridgehead is established.

**Kaladin:** Kaladin takes a proactive role in the battle, leading four other decoys to draw Parshendi arrows away from the vulnerable bridge crews. He skillfully attracts the most attention and the majority of the arrows, ensuring the bridges are placed successfully for the army to cross. Afterward, he tends to Teft and Skar, who have been wounded, performing surgery on Teft under the protection of surrounding soldiers.

**Dalinar:** Dalinar orders his men to set a bridge for crossing and charges into the fray with Adolin. During the skirmish, he is targeted by Parshendi wielding large rocks but dispatches them with ease. As the battle progresses, Dalinar experiences the Thrill—a euphoric battle state—but it fades when he confronts a youthful Parshendi, prompting him to question the morality of the battle. His thoughts drift to the Codes, Gavilar’s murder, and his prophetic visions. Adolin's warning about additional Parshendi forces interrupts his contemplation. Climbing a rock formation, Dalinar discovers that Sadeas has betrayed him, retreating with his army and bridge crews, leaving Dalinar's forces isolated and surrounded.

## Rowan's "If Asked" Notes

**Q: What are the Codes that Dalinar follows?**
A: The Codes are a set of rules for conduct that emphasize honor, integrity, and leading by example. Dalinar believes in not asking his men to endure hardships he wouldn't face himself.

**Q: Why does Kaladin lead a decoy operation?**
A: Kaladin's strategy is to draw Parshendi fire away from the vulnerable bridge crews, allowing them to safely place the bridges needed for the army's advance.

**Q: What is the significance of the Thrill for Dalinar?**
A: The Thrill is a euphoric state during battle that can drive soldiers to perform extraordinary feats. For Dalinar, it raises questions about his motivations and the morality of warfare.

**Q: Why is Sadeas’s retreat significant?**
A: Sadeas’s retreat is a betrayal that leaves Dalinar’s forces surrounded by the enemy, dramatically altering the battle’s dynamics and leading to severe consequences.

**Q: How does Dalinar feel about fighting the Parshendi?**
A: Dalinar is conflicted; while adept at combat, he questions the morality of the conflict, especially after experiencing visions and considering Gavilar's death.

**Q: What role does Adolin play in this chapter?**
A: Adolin fights alongside Dalinar and alerts him to new enemy movements, showing his reliability and warrior prowess.

**Q: How does Kaladin tend to wounded soldiers?**
A: Kaladin uses his medical skills to perform surgery on Teft, showcasing his care for his comrades and his leadership within Bridge Four.

**Q: Why does Dalinar climb the rock formation?**
A: Dalinar climbs to assess the battlefield and discovers Sadeas’s betrayal, realizing that his forces have been abandoned and are now surrounded.
