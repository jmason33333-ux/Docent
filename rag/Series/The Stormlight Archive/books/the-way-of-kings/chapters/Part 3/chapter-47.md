## Quick Summary
**One Year Ago**

Kaladin, a squadleader in the Alethi army, is nearing the end of his enlistment. Despite this, he has decided to re-enlist due to his inability to face his family after failing to protect his younger brother, Tien. In an effort to better protect his squad, Kaladin bribes another squadleader to transfer Cenn, a new recruit, into his group. Kaladin is deeply troubled by the Alethi's internal conflicts and the constant battles they wage among themselves, even as they face real external threats.

As the chapter unfolds, Kaladin ensures his men will receive prompt medical attention by bribing the surgeons, a gesture that highlights his leadership and care for his squad. When he meets his squad, Cenn's resemblance to Tien unsettles him. The squad is soon called to battle, and Kaladin, alongside his second, Dallet, leads them forward.

During the battle, Kaladin spots Cenn in danger and bravely fights through six opponents to reach him. As other squad members arrive, Kaladin bandages Cenn's wounded leg. Seizing the opportunity to earn a place on the Shattered Plains, Kaladin targets a lighteyed officer on horseback and quickly dispatches him.

The battle takes a dire turn when a Shardbearer in golden Shardplate appears, trampling Cenn and threatening Amaram, the high-ranking officer. Despite the chaos, Kaladin and his men charge the Shardbearer, but they are largely ineffective against the formidable foe. The Shardbearer effortlessly cuts down most of Kaladin's squad. In a desperate move, Kaladin attacks the Shardbearer again, and with a remarkable display of skill, catches the severed spearhead mid-air and thrusts it into the Shardbearer's eye slit, killing him.

Kaladin's victory is bittersweet. Although he has the right to claim the Shardblade and become a lighteyes, he refuses it, feeling it would corrupt him. Instead, he offers the weapon to Coreb, one of the few surviving members of his squad.

## Rowan's "If Asked" Notes

**Q: Why does Kaladin refuse the Shardblade?**
A: Kaladin refuses the Shardblade because accepting it would elevate him to the rank of a lighteyes, a group he views as corrupt and responsible for much of the suffering he's witnessed. He prioritizes his personal integrity over the power and status that would come with the Shardblade.

**Q: How did Kaladin kill the Shardbearer?**
A: In a moment of quick thinking and exceptional skill, Kaladin catches the spearhead of his broken weapon mid-air and thrusts it into the eye slit of the Shardbearer's helmet, a vulnerable point in the otherwise impenetrable armor.

**Q: What is the significance of Cenn's character?**
A: Cenn's character serves as a poignant reminder of Kaladin's brother Tien, emphasizing Kaladin's protective instincts and the emotional burden he carries from past failures.

**Q: Why does Kaladin feel guilty about Tien?**
A: Kaladin feels responsible for Tien's death in a previous battle, believing he failed to protect his younger brother. This guilt drives many of his actions and decisions in the army.

**Q: What motivates Kaladin to re-enlist?**
A: Kaladin chooses to re-enlist because he cannot face his family after failing to protect Tien, and he feels a responsibility to continue fighting to prevent others from experiencing similar losses.

**Q: How does Kaladin's leadership manifest in this chapter?**
A: Kaladin's leadership is evident through his protective actions, such as arranging for his squad's medical care and his strategic decisions during battle to safeguard his men.

**Q: What are the social implications of becoming a lighteyes?**
A: Becoming a lighteyes would grant Kaladin elevated social status and power, but it would also align him with a group he views as morally corrupt and responsible for societal injustices.

**Q: How does the battle reflect the Alethi's internal conflicts?**
A: The battle highlights the Alethi's tendency to fight among themselves, even in the face of external threats, showcasing the internal divisions and power struggles within their society.
