## Quick Summary

**Five and a half years ago**

Brightlord Roshone and his son Rillir are attacked by a whitespine during a hunt. Rillir is severely injured, and Lirin, the village surgeon, attempts to save him. However, realizing Rillir's wounds are beyond help, Lirin prioritizes saving Roshone. Roshone insists that Lirin should focus on his son, but Lirin adheres to the guidelines of triage, explaining that a surgeon must know when a patient is beyond help. While stitching up Roshone, Lirin's knife comes close to a major artery, causing him to hesitate but ultimately deciding to save Roshone's life despite his personal feelings.

Later, Kaladin questions Lirin about his decision not to let Roshone die, noting it would have resolved many of their problems. Lirin explains that murder is not an option for him, emphasizing the need to care for life even if the lighteyes do not. This conversation leads Kaladin to a realization: in surgery, some parts beyond repair must be removed, and similarly, some people might need to be removed for the greater good.
