## Quick Summary
**One year ago**

Kaladin is in Amaram's camp, sitting in a waiting room, grappling with the aftermath of a battle where only four of his twenty-six men survived. Distressed and numb, he reflects on the irony of being considered lucky for surviving such losses. Kaladin, who has killed a Shardbearer, is offered the Shards—a rare and valuable prize—but he refuses them, feeling both revolted by the idea and disdainful of becoming a lighteyes.

Amaram enters with a Stormwarden, discussing the identity and motives of the Shardbearer Kaladin killed. Kaladin's squad, along with some lighteyed officers, gather in the room. Amaram questions Kaladin, probing into why he rejected the Shards, but Kaladin avoids revealing his aversion to lighteyes by claiming ignorance of his own motives.

In a shocking betrayal, Amaram signals his soldiers, who slaughter Kaladin's surviving men while Kaladin is restrained. Despite showing some guilt, Amaram rationalizes his betrayal, claiming that Kaladin would eventually demand the Shards back and that Amaram, trained in swordsmanship, deserves them more. To protect Kaladin for saving his life, Amaram spares him but brands him a slave, fabricating a story that credits himself with the Shardbearer's death and claims Kaladin fled the scene.

## Rowan's "If Asked" Notes

**Q: Why does Kaladin refuse the Shards?**
A: Kaladin refuses the Shards because accepting them would turn him into a lighteyes, a transformation he finds revolting. His disdain for lighteyes, rooted in their perceived corruption and arrogance, outweighs the allure of power and status that the Shards represent.

**Q: What motivates Amaram to betray Kaladin?**
A: Amaram is driven by ambition and the desire to possess the Shards, which symbolize immense power and prestige. He rationalizes his betrayal by believing that his rank and swordsmanship make him more deserving of the Shards.

**Q: How does Amaram justify killing Kaladin's men?**
A: Amaram claims that sparing Kaladin is a reward for saving his life, but he believes Kaladin would eventually want the Shards back. The murder of Kaladin's men is framed as necessary to maintain this fabricated narrative.

**Q: What is the significance of Kaladin's branding?**
A: Kaladin's branding as a slave marks a pivotal turn in his life, symbolizing his fall from a respected squad leader to a dehumanized position. This event fuels his animosity towards lighteyes and sets the stage for his future struggles.

**Q: Who are the Shardbearers mentioned by Kaladin?**
A: Lanacin the Surefooted and Evod Markmaker are noted Shardbearers, mentioned as part of Kaladin’s reflections on the rarity and prestige of killing such individuals. Their mention underscores the magnitude of his achievement.

**Q: What role does Tien play in this chapter?**
A: Tien is mentioned as part of Kaladin's internal reflections, representing his familial connections and deeper motivations. Tien's memory influences Kaladin's decisions and emotional state throughout his ordeal.

**Q: Why does Kaladin feel numb after the battle?**
A: The overwhelming loss of his men and the moral implications of his victory leave Kaladin feeling detached and emotionally exhausted. He struggles with the paradox of being labeled lucky despite his profound losses.

**Q: How does this chapter set up future events for Kaladin?**
A: The betrayal by Amaram and Kaladin's branding establish critical themes of trust, betrayal, and identity that will continue to shape his journey. These events deepen his distrust of lighteyes and his resolve to survive and protect others.
