## Quick Summary
Kaladin awakes with a sense of despair, haunted by the thought that as bridgemen, they are merely bait meant to die in battle. Despite his dread, he joins his men, who are organized and ready for practice. However, their routine is interrupted by the appearance of Brightlord Matal and his wife, Hashal, who has taken over Lamaril's role. Hashal dismissively tells Kaladin that he has been given another chance to prove his worth only as a bridgeman. When Kaladin questions how Matal ended up in his position, Hashal instructs a soldier to beat Kaladin as punishment. Kaladin briefly considers fighting back but chooses to let the spear strike him, symbolizing his resignation to their grim situation. Hashal then informs the bridgemen that they will be assigned to chasm duty exclusively.

Kaladin and his men descend into the chasms, where Kaladin grapples with his frustration and Syl's reminders of his survival in the highstorm. Syl's mention of killing in the past shocks Kaladin, but she asserts that sometimes killing is necessary. In the chasm, Rock inquires about their next move, but Kaladin, overcome with hopelessness, claims that they have no fight left. As they begin scavenging bodies, Teft challenges Kaladin's despair by quoting the Radiant oath, "journey before destination," encouraging him not to give up. Inspired by this and Syl's support, Kaladin decides to rally his men again, proposing that their best chance is to escape. He offers to train them as spearmen to improve their odds, and all agree except Rock, who prefers to cook.

## Rowan's "If Asked" Notes

**Q: Why is Kaladin so despondent at the start of the chapter?**  
A: Kaladin feels overwhelmed by the apparent futility of their situation, believing that the bridgemen are destined to die and that their efforts are meaningless.

**Q: Who are Brightlord Matal and Hashal?**  
A: Matal and Hashal are the new overseers of the bridgemen, replacing Lamaril. Hashal is the more vocal and authoritative of the two.

**Q: What prompts Kaladin to change his mind about giving up?**  
A: Teft's reminder of the Radiant oath and Syl's encouragement push Kaladin to reconsider his hopelessness and decide to train the men for escape.

**Q: Why does Rock refuse to fight?**  
A: Rock believes that fighting is beneath him due to his cultural background, and instead offers to support the group by cooking.

**Q: What does Syl mean when she mentions killing is sometimes right?**  
A: Syl's comment hints at her complex nature, suggesting that her understanding of morality is broader than it initially seems.

**Q: What is the significance of Teft quoting 'journey before destination'?**  
A: This line is part of the Radiant oath, emphasizing the importance of the path and actions taken, not just the end goal.

**Q: Why are the bridgemen now assigned solely to chasm duty?**  
A: Hashal orders this to keep them occupied and away from battle, reducing their risk of immediate death but still condemning them to a dangerous task.

**Q: Why does Kaladin decide to train the bridgemen as spearmen?**  
A: He believes that improving their combat skills might increase their chances of escaping their dire circumstances.
