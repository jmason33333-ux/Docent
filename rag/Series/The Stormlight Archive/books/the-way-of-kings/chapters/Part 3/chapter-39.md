## Quick Summary

**Shallan Davar:**

Shallan is consumed by unease as she sits in her room, fixated on sketching the deaths of the four robbers from a few nights prior. Despite not consciously recalling these events, her hands seem to work of their own accord, capturing the harrowing scene. As she draws, Shallan contemplates deep philosophical questions, reflecting on Jasnah's controversial decision to kill the robbers and the ethical implications it carries. Her thoughts drift to her own moral quandaries surrounding the theft of Jasnah's Soulcaster.

Suddenly, Shallan's sketches morph into a different, unsettling image: a richly-dressed man lying lifeless in a pool of blood. This unexpected vision shakes her, prompting her to flee the room in a panic. A servant encounters her in the hallway, handing her a flashing spanreed. Retreating to the main room, she engages in communication with her brother, Balat. He expresses concern over recent visits from their father's ominous "friends," heightening Shallan's anxiety regarding the stolen Soulcaster.

To quell her fears, especially when a chambermaid enters their quarters, Shallan secures the Soulcaster in her safe-pouch. She leaves the room with a basket containing bread and Bluebar jam, a gift from Kabsal, and seeks solace in the palace gardens.

Two hours later, Shallan has calmed herself by drawing the serene life of snails and plants in the gardens. She reflects on the symbiotic relationship she observes between a snail and the shalebark it inhabits, noting her thoughts beside the drawing. Ensuring her solitude, she retrieves the Soulcaster, attempting to decipher its mechanics since Luesh, the family's steward, can no longer guide her. Frustration mounts as her efforts prove fruitless, leaving her to ponder alternative strategies to aid her family.
