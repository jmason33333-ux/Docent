## Quick Summary
Kaladin continues to adapt and improve the strategies of Bridge Four. Lopen, a new recruit, joins the crew, and along with Dabbid, he brings a water cart for the first time, a small but significant improvement in their harsh conditions.

As they approach the Parshendi position, Kaladin notices that the enemy is already prepared and strategically positioned. He assesses their situation and realizes that they risk losing more men, which could cause the bridge to topple and crush the crew. To mitigate this danger, he orders Bridge Four to employ a new technique—side-carrying the bridge. This strategy is innovative and risky but aims to protect his men from the lethal barrage of arrows.

The side-carry is successful, and for the first time, Bridge Four completes an assault without losing any members. However, this success comes at a cost. While Bridge Four survives, the other bridge crews suffer heavy casualties. Most of the other bridges fall due to concentrated Parshendi fire or their failed attempts to emulate Bridge Four's technique. As a result, Sadeas's forces become disorganized and are forced to retreat, their assault undermined by the unexpected maneuver.

After the retreat, Lamaril and Gaz confront Kaladin, ready to execute him for his insubordination and the failure of the assault. Kaladin, however, manages to convince them that his skills are necessary to pacify Sadeas. Instead of execution, Lamaril orders Kaladin to be beaten in front of Bridge Four as punishment. During the beating, Kaladin's belt pouch is kicked, scattering his dun spheres on the ground, symbolizing not only his physical defeat but also the loss of his hard-earned resources.
