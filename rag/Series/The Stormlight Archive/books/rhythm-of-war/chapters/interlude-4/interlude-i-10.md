## Quick Summary
Hesina makes notes on the maps Rlain has smuggled to them in the surgery enclosure in the Radiant infirmary. Lirin comes in and discusses her parents with her until the topic causes Hesina to bring up Kaladin. Lirin stiffly defends his attitude towards his son, and the argument grows intense enough that he makes to leave the enclosure. Hesina stops him and, frustrated, tries to point out to Lirin that Kaladin is probably the person they could most want to be a soldier, since there is going to be fighting regardless. When she points to the help in the room that day, all of whom have shash glyphs painted on their foreheads, and asks him what he sees, he says that he sees fools.

In reply, Hesina leads Lirin over to Noril and initiates a conversation between the two of them. Noril tells Lirin that none of the treatment he has ever received from ardents has helped, but the thought of Kaladin does. Hesina asks Lirin to speak with each of the people in the room, asking why they wear the shash glyph and really seeing them.

## Rowan's "If Asked" Notes

**Q: What are the maps?**  
A: Hesina makes notes on maps that Rlain has smuggled to them in the surgery enclosure in the Radiant infirmary. These are likely maps of the tower or plans for the rescue attempt or escape. They're being used to plan something, possibly the rescue of the Radiants.

**Q: What is the shash glyph?**  
A: All of the help in the room have shash glyphs painted on their foreheads. The shash glyph is a symbol that means "dangerous" or "to be watched." It's what was painted on Kaladin's forehead when he was a slave. These people are wearing it as a symbol of solidarity or to show they're with Kaladin.

**Q: Why does Lirin see them as fools?**  
A: When Hesina asks Lirin what he sees when he looks at the people with shash glyphs, he says he sees fools. This shows Lirin's perspective—he doesn't understand why they would wear the symbol or what it means to them. He's not seeing them as people who are helped by Kaladin.

**Q: What does Hesina want Lirin to understand?**  
A: Hesina tries to point out to Lirin that Kaladin is probably the person they could most want to be a soldier, since there is going to be fighting regardless. She wants him to see that Kaladin's role as a soldier and protector is valuable and necessary, even if Lirin doesn't approve of violence.

**Q: What does Noril tell Lirin?**  
A: Noril tells Lirin that none of the treatment he has ever received from ardents has helped, but the thought of Kaladin does. This shows how Kaladin helps people—not just through medical treatment, but through his presence, his understanding, and his support. This is what Hesina wants Lirin to see.

**Q: What does Hesina ask Lirin to do?**  
A: Hesina asks Lirin to speak with each of the people in the room, asking why they wear the shash glyph and really seeing them. She wants him to understand who these people are, why they support Kaladin, and how Kaladin helps them. This is about Lirin learning to see people for who they are, not just as "fools."

**Q: Why is this interlude important?**  
A: This interlude is important because it shows the conflict between Lirin and Hesina about Kaladin, and it shows how Kaladin helps people in ways that Lirin doesn't understand. It's about perspective and understanding, and about seeing the value in what Kaladin does, even if it involves violence.

**Q: What is the significance of the shash glyph?**  
A: The shash glyph is significant because it's what was painted on Kaladin's forehead when he was a slave. These people are wearing it as a symbol of solidarity with Kaladin and to show that they're with him. It's a way of showing support and understanding, even if Lirin doesn't see it that way.
