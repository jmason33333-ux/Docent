## Quick Summary
Adin is the son of a potter in the tower, and he is determined to be a Windrunner. He knows he has to be brave in order for a spren to choose him, and he's on the lookout for the right moment. He makes his way through the tower with his father toward the shop where his father works, noting a disturbance in the atrium. Once they arrive at the shop, he helps his father with the clay. The shop owner enters and says that the disturbance in the atrium has him worried and that they're going to close the shop today, leaving them to continue to make pots. Once the owner leaves, Adin's father leaves as well, telling Adin to watch the shop. Adin realizes that his father is going to watch over the Radiants, and he asks to go with him, and his father relents.

**Q: Why does Adin's father leave?**  
A: Once the shop owner leaves, Adin's father leaves as well, telling Adin to watch the shop. Adin realizes that his father is going to watch over the Radiants. This shows that people in the tower are helping to protect the unconscious Radiants.

**Q: Why does Adin want to go with his father?**  
A: Adin asks to go with his father, and his father relents. Adin wants to be brave and help, and he sees this as an opportunity to be brave and potentially be noticed by a spren. This is part of his determination to become a Windrunner.

**Q: What does it take to become a Windrunner?**  
A: Adin knows he has to be brave in order for a spren to choose him. He's on the lookout for the right moment to show his bravery. This shows his understanding of what it takes to become a Radiant—not just wanting it, but being brave and ready when the moment comes.

**Q: Why is this interlude important?**  
A: This interlude is important because it shows the perspective of ordinary people in the tower during the occupation, and it shows how people are helping to protect the Radiants. It also shows Adin's determination and hope, which represents the hope that people still have despite the occupation.

**Q: What is the significance of watching over the Radiants?**  
A: Watching over the Radiants is important because they're unconscious and vulnerable. People like Adin's father are helping to protect them from the Fused and Regals who might want to harm them. This is part of the resistance effort during the occupation.

**Q: Will Adin become a Windrunner?**  
A: The chapter doesn't reveal whether Adin becomes a Windrunner, but it shows his determination and his understanding of what it takes. He's looking for the right moment to be brave, and going with his father to watch over the Radiants might be that moment.
