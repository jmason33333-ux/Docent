## Quick Summary
Kaladin and Teft go looking for Noril, the missing refugee. The ardent at the first devotary tells them Noril tried to kill himself, so they sent him to the Devotary of Mercy. Following directions, they find it on the fourth floor. The ardent there refuses to divulge patient information until Teft summons Phendorana and proves they have sufficient rank. After looking up the room number, the ardent leads them to Noril. On the way, both Kaladin and Teft are disillusioned seeing how all patients are locked up in the dark. They convince Noril to come out to a balcony to talk, and when they prove they understand how he feels, Noril begins to open up. Kuno, shocked to see Noril speaking, discusses the standard of care for mental illness with Kaladin. He admits they need to do better but sees no way to change the system. Kaladin asks that Noril be released into his care and warns Kuno that he will be back for the others.

## Rowan's "If Asked" Notes

**Q: Why are patients locked up in the dark?**
A: The Devotary of Mercy treats patients with mental illness by locking them up in dark rooms. This is the standard of care, and it shocks both Kaladin and Teft when they see it. Kuno admits the system needs to change but feels powerless to change it. This treatment is clearly inadequate and harmful.

**Q: Why did Noril try to kill himself?**
A: Noril is a one-armed Alethi refugee who has been struggling. The first devotary tells Kaladin and Teft that Noril tried to kill himself, which is why he was sent to the Devotary of Mercy. When Kaladin and Teft talk with him and prove they understand how he feels, Noril begins to open up, suggesting that being understood and having support helps.

**Q: What does Kaladin mean when he says he'll be back for the others?**
A: After seeing how patients are treated and taking Noril into his care, Kaladin warns Kuno that he will be back for the other patients. This suggests Kaladin plans to help more people with mental illness, possibly by taking them into his care or working to change the system. It shows his commitment to helping those who are suffering.

**Q: Why does Teft summon Phendorana?**
A: The ardent at the Devotary of Mercy refuses to give out patient information. Teft summons his spren Phendorana to prove that he and Kaladin are Knights Radiant with sufficient rank to authorize the request. This shows that their Radiant status gives them authority to access information they need.

**Q: How do Kaladin and Teft help Noril?**
A: They convince Noril to come out to a balcony to talk. When they prove they understand how he feels (likely through their own experiences with depression and trauma), Noril begins to open up. This shows the power of empathy and understanding in helping those who are struggling. Kaladin then takes Noril into his care, getting him out of the dark locked room.

**Q: What is the significance of this chapter?**
A: This chapter highlights the poor treatment of mental illness in Alethi society and shows Kaladin taking action to help. It connects to his own struggles with depression and shows his growth in wanting to help others who are suffering. It also sets up potential future action as Kaladin promises to return for other patients.
