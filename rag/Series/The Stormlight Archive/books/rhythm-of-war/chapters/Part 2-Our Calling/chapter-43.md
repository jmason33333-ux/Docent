## Quick Summary
Kaladin is in the clinic, where a messenger woman tells them that Navani has surrendered, so they are to live under singer law and not resist. Lirin says that it wasn't so bad before, and it won't be so bad now. Kaladin is frustrated at his response and how they have to live under the rule of the enemy. Hesina and Laral go to check on the people, and Kaladin and his father continue to argue about resisting on the way to their rooms. A little later, Syl alerts them to a search party that is nearing, and Kaladin hides. The soldiers enter and say that they are to take the Radiants into custody. Kaladin overhears and cannot bear the thought of them taking Teft. He emerges to find a Regal and a regular soldier, with the soldier carrying Teft. Kaladin pushes his father into another room as he tells them to leave Teft. The Regal refuses, and they fight brutally, with Kaladin leaving the Regal to bleed out. Kaladin tells the other soldier to leave and then checks on his father, who is in shock. Kaladin says that they all need to go into hiding, and Lirin, furious, refuses. He cannot believe that Kaladin murdered someone in his clinic, his home. He tells Kaladin to leave and that he will stay, despairing because he believes his son has become a monster. Kaladin grabs Teft and leaves.

## Rowan's "If Asked" Notes

**Q: Why does Kaladin kill the Regal?**
A: When soldiers come to take Radiants into custody, Kaladin cannot bear the thought of them taking Teft. He emerges and tells them to leave Teft, but the Regal refuses. They fight brutally, and Kaladin leaves the Regal to bleed out. This happens in Lirin's clinic, his home, which makes it even more shocking to Lirin.

**Q: Why does Lirin call Kaladin a monster?**
A: After Kaladin kills the Regal in Lirin's clinic, Lirin is in shock and furious. He cannot believe that Kaladin murdered someone in his clinic, his home. He tells Kaladin to leave and says he will stay, despairing because he believes his son has become a monster. This is a fundamental disagreement about violence and morality.

**Q: Why does Lirin say it wasn't so bad before?**
A: When they learn that Navani has surrendered and they're to live under singer law, Lirin says that it wasn't so bad before (referring to when singers ruled before) and it won't be so bad now. This shows his acceptance and his belief that resistance isn't worth the cost. Kaladin is frustrated by this response.

**Q: Why does Kaladin leave?**
A: After the fight and Lirin's reaction, Kaladin says they all need to go into hiding, but Lirin refuses. Lirin tells Kaladin to leave, and Kaladin grabs Teft and leaves. This splits the family—Kaladin and Teft go into hiding, while Lirin stays. This is a painful separation driven by fundamental differences in their beliefs.

**Q: What is the significance of this chapter?**
A: This chapter represents a breaking point in Kaladin's relationship with his father. Kaladin's need to protect Teft and resist the occupation conflicts with Lirin's pacifism and acceptance. The brutal fight in Lirin's clinic, his home, and Lirin's declaration that Kaladin is a monster, creates an irreparable split between them. Kaladin must now operate separately from his family.

**Q: Why can't Kaladin bear them taking Teft?**
A: Teft is Kaladin's friend and member of Bridge Four. He's unconscious and vulnerable. Kaladin has been working to help people like Teft (the group therapy sessions), and the thought of the invaders taking him into custody (likely for experiments or worse) is unbearable. This drives him to fight despite Lirin's pacifist philosophy.
