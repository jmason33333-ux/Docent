## Quick Summary
Taking Lirin's advice, Kaladin begins setting up group therapy sessions only for Noril and six other similarly afflicted men from the sanitarium. They sit on the balcony talking about their lives while Kaladin looks on. Hesina, who is taking notes, remarks on how extraordinary it is to her that this helps them, as it runs contrary to all previous treatment advice. Kaladin grows uncomfortable when he realizes she is pitying him for how he felt as a slave. While he appreciates that she thinks she really understands, he knows that what is helping the group of men is to have a support group who has experienced the same feelings.

## Rowan's "If Asked" Notes

**Q: What is group therapy?**
A: Taking Lirin's advice, Kaladin begins setting up group therapy sessions for Noril and six other men from the sanitarium who are similarly afflicted. They sit on the balcony talking about their lives while Kaladin looks on. This is a new approach to treating mental illness, contrary to all previous treatment advice (which involved locking people up in dark rooms).

**Q: Why does Hesina think it's extraordinary?**
A: Hesina, who is taking notes, remarks on how extraordinary it is that this approach helps the men, as it runs contrary to all previous treatment advice. The old system involved locking patients in dark rooms, so seeing them talk openly and help each other is revolutionary.

**Q: Why is Kaladin uncomfortable with Hesina's pity?**
A: Kaladin grows uncomfortable when he realizes Hesina is pitying him for how he felt as a slave. While he appreciates that she thinks she really understands, he knows that what is actually helping the group of men is to have a support group who has experienced the same feelings—not pity from someone who hasn't experienced it.

**Q: What is the key insight about what helps?**
A: Kaladin realizes that what's helping the men isn't pity or sympathy from those who haven't experienced their struggles, but rather having a support group of people who have experienced the same feelings. This shared experience creates understanding and connection that pity cannot provide.

**Q: Why is this approach different?**
A: This group therapy approach is contrary to all previous treatment advice, which involved isolating patients in dark rooms. Instead, Kaladin brings them together to talk about their lives and share their experiences. This is based on the principle that shared experience and peer support can be more effective than isolation.

**Q: What does this chapter show about Kaladin?**
A: This chapter shows Kaladin's growth as a healer and his understanding of mental health. He recognizes the difference between pity and genuine understanding, and he's innovating new approaches to treatment. It also shows his commitment to helping those who are suffering, continuing from his promise in Chapter 25 to help more patients.
