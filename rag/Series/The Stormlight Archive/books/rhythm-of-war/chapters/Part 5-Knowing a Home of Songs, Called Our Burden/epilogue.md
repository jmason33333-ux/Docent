## Quick Summary
Wit has left the others and is now at the Pinnacle. He does tricks for an "audience," a group of spren corrupted by Sja-anat, and explains the importance of subtlety in magic tricks and storytelling. Design contemplates that Wit's tricks with a coin don't work that well on Roshar because coins aren't used to trade there. Wit eventually tells Design to go bother someone else and moves to Elhokar's sitting room.

Soon after, Odium arrives, shows his rage at Wit's contract, and threatens him. However, Odium also asks Wit what his choice of champion would be, which surprises Wit. As the latter leaves the room and walks in the hallways, he ponders whether or not Rayse is growing more thoughtful. Realizing that Wit might have doubts about his identity, Odium attacks Wit. Odium's voice is thoughtful and soft, and Wit feels true horror as he realizes his enemy isn't Rayse any longer. Odium then destroys some of Wit's memories held in his Breaths, which isn't in violation of the contract.

Wit regains consciousness in the hallway, unaware of what happened, and returns to Elhokar's sitting room where the scene happens once again, Odium playing the angry brute more convincingly this time. Wit leaves after saying a different quip than the previous time but has trouble whistling, the loss of Breaths preventing him from using perfect pitch. He wonders if something is wrong as the inconsistencies linked to his memory loss pile up. However, he considers that this encounter went exactly as planned.

## Rowan's "If Asked" Notes

**Q: Who is Design?**  
A: Design is Wit's spren, corrupted by Sja-anat. She's part of Wit's "audience" at the Pinnacle. She contemplates that Wit's tricks with a coin don't work well on Roshar because coins aren't used to trade there. Design is a corrupted spren that Wit has bonded or is working with.

**Q: Why does Odium attack Wit?**  
A: Realizing that Wit might have doubts about his identity, Odium attacks Wit. Odium's voice is thoughtful and soft, and Wit feels true horror as he realizes his enemy isn't Rayse any longer. Odium then destroys some of Wit's memories held in his Breaths. This is Taravangian (the new Odium) attacking Wit to prevent him from realizing the change.

**Q: What memories does Wit lose?**  
A: Odium destroys some of Wit's memories held in his Breaths. This causes Wit to lose memories of the encounter, making him unaware of what happened. He regains consciousness in the hallway, unaware, and the scene happens again. The loss of Breaths also prevents him from using perfect pitch when whistling.

**Q: Why does the encounter happen twice?**  
A: Wit regains consciousness in the hallway, unaware of what happened, and returns to Elhokar's sitting room where the scene happens once again. Odium plays the angry brute more convincingly this time. This is because Wit lost his memories of the first encounter, so he experiences it again without knowing. It's a time loop or reset caused by the memory loss.

**Q: Why is Wit horrified?**  
A: Wit feels true horror as he realizes his enemy isn't Rayse any longer. This is because Taravangian (the new Odium) is more dangerous than Rayse—he's smarter, more thoughtful, and more capable. Wit realizes that the enemy has changed and become more threatening.

**Q: Why does Odium ask about the champion?**  
A: Odium asks Wit what his choice of champion would be, which surprises Wit. This is Taravangian trying to understand Wit's strategy and thinking. It shows that the new Odium is more thoughtful and strategic than Rayse was.

**Q: Why is this epilogue called "Dirty Tricks"?**  
A: The title "Dirty Tricks" refers to Wit's magic tricks and storytelling, but also to Odium's trick—attacking Wit and destroying his memories without violating the contract. It's about deception and manipulation, and about the "dirty tricks" that both sides are playing.

**Q: What does the endnote mean?**  
A: The endnote is a ketek (a palindrome poem) written by El, a Fused scholar, to commemorate the restoration of the Sibling. It reads: "Burdens, Our Calling. Songs of Home, a knowledge: Knowing a Home of Songs, called our burden." This is a poetic summary of the book's five parts, written in the form of a ketek.

**Q: What does this mean for the future?**  
A: Wit losing his memories and not realizing that Odium has changed is a major problem. Taravangian is now Odium and is more dangerous than Rayse, but Wit doesn't remember this. This sets up major challenges for the future, as Wit won't be able to warn others or adjust his strategy accordingly.
