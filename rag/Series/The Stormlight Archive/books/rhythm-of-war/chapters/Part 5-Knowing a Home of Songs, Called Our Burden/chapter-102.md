## Quick Summary
**Venli:** As Venli and her group of friends wait for an Oathgate transfer to Kholinar, Timbre expresses her disappointment that Venli is abandoning the Radiants. Venli goes to the front of the line to investigate what is taking so long and discovers that Vyre has left, taking the only Blade able to work the Oathgates with him. She convinces everyone that there is a highstorm coming soon, moving them all inside. On the way, Venli determines to smuggle Kaladin and his family out with them; the group agrees that this is a good idea, so she leaves for the atrium.

**Navani:** Navani, having spent all night listening as the singers discovered the tone for anti-Stormlight, numbly considers her failure in handing such a weapon to their enemies. She paints a glyph for divine direction, contemplating her belief in the Almighty. Moash arrives with the intention of killing Navani, but Raboniel negotiates with him until he agrees to get rid of Kaladin first. Leaving with a sack of sand and a dagger designed to kill a spren as well as instructions to break the final node, Moash halts when Navani calls him a traitor and replies to her coldly. When he leaves, Navani's guard brings her a candle to burn her prayers. After they are burnt, she discovers the dagger Raboniel had used to kill her daughter, still charged with a little bit of anti-Voidlight.

**Kaladin:** Kaladin strides towards the atrium, determined to live up to his reputation one last time and followed by a crowd of expectant humans and singers. He and Syl reunite near the Breakaway, and they move toward the atrium together to fight the Pursuer. The atrium empties as he arrives, leaving a clear line of sight to the Pursuer, who streaks towards him.

## Rowan's "If Asked" Notes

**Q: Why did Vyre leave?**  
A: Venli discovers that Vyre (Moash) has left, taking the only Blade able to work the Oathgates with him. This means they can't use the Oathgates to escape. Vyre likely left to go after Kaladin, as Raboniel negotiated with him to get rid of Kaladin first instead of killing Navani.

**Q: What is anti-Stormlight?**  
A: Navani spent all night listening as the singers discovered the tone for anti-Stormlight. This is the opposite vibration that nullifies Stormlight, similar to how anti-Voidlight nullifies Voidlight. This is a dangerous weapon that can kill spren and Radiants.

**Q: Why does Moash go after Kaladin?**  
A: Moash arrives with the intention of killing Navani, but Raboniel negotiates with him until he agrees to get rid of Kaladin first. Moash leaves with a sack of sand and a dagger designed to kill a spren, as well as instructions to break the final node. This is part of the enemy's plan to eliminate Kaladin and destroy the tower's defenses.

**Q: What does Navani discover?**  
A: After burning her prayers, Navani discovers the dagger Raboniel had used to kill her daughter, still charged with a little bit of anti-Voidlight. This is significant because it means Navani has a weapon that can kill Fused and end their rebirth cycle, just like Raboniel used it to free her daughter.

**Q: Why does Venli decide to help Kaladin?**  
A: On the way inside (after convincing everyone a highstorm is coming), Venli determines to smuggle Kaladin and his family out with them. The group agrees this is a good idea. This shows Venli's growth—she's choosing to help instead of just escaping.

**Q: What happens when Kaladin and Syl reunite?**  
A: Kaladin and Syl reunite near the Breakaway, and they move toward the atrium together to fight the Pursuer. This is a moment of connection and strength for Kaladin, as he regains his bond with Syl and prepares for the final confrontation.

**Q: Why is this chapter called "Highstorm Coming"?**  
A: The title "Highstorm Coming" refers to Venli's excuse to get everyone inside (she convinces them a highstorm is coming), but it also symbolizes the coming confrontation—the storm of battle that's about to break. It's about the approaching climax and danger.

**Q: What does the epigraph mean?**  
A: The epigraph is from a singer's perspective, saying that to humans, their visages become symbols, and you find echoes of it in art from centuries before the Return. This relates to how singers are depicted in human art and culture, and how those depictions shape perception and understanding.
