## Quick Summary
Jasnah relaxes in her tent with Wit just after receiving word of the coalition's victory in Emul. She sorts through her notes from interviews with the Heralds, trying to find something to help with the situation at Urithiru, and admits her worry to Wit that Odium can see the future while they can't. Wit replies that they need to make sure Odium can't win even if he is sure not to lose. He presents to her his draft of a contract between Dalinar and Odium, explaining that one of Rayse's weaknesses is that he wants not only to win but to win in a way that shows off his intelligence. Wit states that the contest of champions will be no simple duel, but a matter of the heart of each champion; when asked if she trusts the hearts of those she fights alongside, Jasnah has no answer. She asks Wit who he really is, and he tells her that he is a man who is not bound.

## Rowan's "If Asked" Notes

**Q: What is the contract?**  
A: Wit presents to Jasnah his draft of a contract between Dalinar and Odium. This is the agreement that will govern the contest of champions. Wit explains that one of Rayse's weaknesses is that he wants not only to win but to win in a way that shows off his intelligence, which can be used in the contract.

**Q: What does Wit mean about Odium not being able to win?**  
A: Wit replies that they need to make sure Odium can't win even if he is sure not to lose. This means that even if Odium can see the future and knows he won't lose, they need to structure the contract so that he can't actually win—perhaps by making it a draw or by ensuring the outcome doesn't favor him.

**Q: What is the contest of champions really?**  
A: Wit states that the contest of champions will be no simple duel, but a matter of the heart of each champion. This means it's not just about physical combat, but about the character, motivations, and choices of the champions. The heart of each champion will determine the outcome.

**Q: Why does Jasnah have no answer?**  
A: When asked if she trusts the hearts of those she fights alongside, Jasnah has no answer. This shows her uncertainty about whether the people on their side have the right hearts, motivations, and character to win the contest. It's a moment of doubt about their champions.

**Q: What does Wit mean by "not bound"?**  
A: When Jasnah asks Wit who he really is, he tells her that he is a man who is not bound. This is a significant revelation about Wit's nature—he's not bound by the same rules, oaths, or constraints as others. This relates to his role as a Worldhopper and his ability to move between worlds and systems.

**Q: What is Jasnah worried about?**  
A: Jasnah admits her worry to Wit that Odium can see the future while they can't. This is a significant disadvantage—Odium can see possible outcomes and plan accordingly, while they're operating blind. This makes it very difficult to outmaneuver him.

**Q: What does the epigraph mean?**  
A: The epigraph is from someone who had their title and rhythms stripped from them for insisting that something should not be killed, but should instead be reconditioned and repurposed. This may relate to spren, Radiants, or some other group. It's about trying to find another way instead of destruction.

**Q: Why is this chapter called "Not Bound"?**  
A: The title "Not Bound" refers to Wit's revelation that he is a man who is not bound. It's about freedom from constraints, rules, and limitations. It also relates to the idea that the contest of champions is not bound by simple rules—it's about the heart, not just physical combat.
