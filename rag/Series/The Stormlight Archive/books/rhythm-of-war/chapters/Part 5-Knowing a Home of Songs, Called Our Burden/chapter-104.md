## Quick Summary
**Rlain:** Rlain and Dabbid are challenged by some guards as they make their way to Navani and the Sibling. The guards don't believe they are delivering water to the scholars once they realize who Rlain is, so they lead the two to their superior for confirmation that they are allowed access to the basement.

**Kaladin:** Doing his best to project confidence, Kaladin grapples with Lezian's third body. Wrestling on the ground again, Kaladin loudly proclaims that he does not fear the Pursuer because he knows how to face death; the Pursuer, on the other hand, fears Kaladin precisely because Kaladin does not fear him. By pulling out another knife, he tricks Lezian into forming his fourth body.

**Venli:** Venli makes her way to the front of the crowd in the atrium and watches the contest for a bit before locating Leshwi. As she reaches the Fused, Leshwi points out that Lezian is in his final body and cannot retreat to renew himself because of the pressure of the audience.

**Teft:** Teft leads Moash on a chase through the infirmary until Moash kills one of the unconscious Radiants using Jezrien's Honorblade. To keep Moash from attacking Lift next, Teft provokes a one-on-one confrontation instead. Moash easily wounds Teft and sends him to the ground, but Teft stops the next killing blow by straining together with Phendorana and manifesting her partially as a spear shaft. Back up on his knees, Teft prepares to grapple with Moash; Moash instead stabs Phendorana with the anti-Stormlight dagger from Raboniel. Teft, unable to move from the pain and horror of Phendorana's death, tells Moash that he knows he is loved as his former friend picks him up and then stabs him with Jezrien's Honorblade, killing him.

## Rowan's "If Asked" Notes

**Q: How does Kaladin defeat the Pursuer?**  
A: Kaladin loudly proclaims that he does not fear the Pursuer because he knows how to face death; the Pursuer, on the other hand, fears Kaladin precisely because Kaladin does not fear him. By pulling out another knife, he tricks Lezian into forming his fourth body. The Pursuer can only have four bodies, so this is his final form—if he dies now, he dies permanently.

**Q: What happens when the Pursuer uses his fourth body?**  
A: Leshwi points out that Lezian is in his final body and cannot retreat to renew himself because of the pressure of the audience. This means if Kaladin kills him now, he dies permanently. The Pursuer is trapped and cannot escape.

**Q: Why does Teft manifest Phendorana as a spear?**  
A: Moash easily wounds Teft and sends him to the ground, but Teft stops the next killing blow by straining together with Phendorana and manifesting her partially as a spear shaft. This is a desperate act to save himself, showing the bond between them and Teft's determination to fight.

**Q: What does the anti-Stormlight dagger do?**  
A: Moash stabs Phendorana with the anti-Stormlight dagger from Raboniel. This kills Phendorana permanently—spren killed with anti-Stormlight cannot be revived. This is the same type of weapon that Raboniel used to kill her daughter Essu.

**Q: What are Teft's last words?**  
A: Teft, unable to move from the pain and horror of Phendorana's death, tells Moash that he knows he is loved as his former friend picks him up and then stabs him with Jezrien's Honorblade, killing him. These are powerful last words—Teft dies knowing he is loved, which is a significant moment of growth from his earlier struggles with self-worth.

**Q: Why does Moash kill a Radiant?**  
A: Teft leads Moash on a chase through the infirmary until Moash kills one of the unconscious Radiants using Jezrien's Honorblade. This is to show Moash's cruelty and to demonstrate the danger they're in. It's also to provoke Teft into a direct confrontation.

**Q: Why is this chapter called "Full of Hope"?**  
A: The title "Full of Hope" is ironic given Teft's death, but it also refers to Teft's final words—he dies knowing he is loved, which is a moment of hope and acceptance. It may also refer to the hope that remains despite the loss, or to Kaladin's confidence in facing the Pursuer.

**Q: What does the epigraph mean?**  
A: The epigraph says "Humans are a poem. A song." This relates to the beauty and complexity of humans, and to how they are perceived by singers. It's about the poetic nature of humanity and their place in the world.

**Q: What is the significance of Teft's death?**  
A: Teft's death is significant because he's been a major character throughout the series, and his growth from addiction and self-doubt to knowing he is loved is complete. His death is tragic but also shows his courage and the strength of his bond with Phendorana. It's a moment of both loss and triumph.
