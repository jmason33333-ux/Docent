## Quick Summary
Dalinar and the Mink are in a flying platform overseeing the battle between the Alethi and the Vedens. The Vedens are accompanied by some Fused and Skybreakers. A scribe comes with news from Urithiru. The report says that those at the tower accidentally deployed a device that interferes with both Radiant powers and the Oathgates, and that they were only able to deliver this message by hiking out of range. Dalinar and the Mink both suspect that this is intentionally caused by Taravangian as a way to isolate them during his betrayal, though they both believe there is more to the matter. As they continue to survey the battle, Szeth, in disguise at Dalinar's side, grabs and pulls him away as a Skybreaker appears. The Skybreaker turns out to be Nalan (Nale), the Herald, who tells Dalinar that he has to submit because his war is unjust. He asks how Dalinar has access to the surges of Honor, as this should not be possible. Nale attacks, and Dalinar touches him at the Stormfather's urging. In doing so, he forges a Connection with him and sees flashes of his past, along with eight lines of light extending from him into the distance. He realizes that the lines are the Oathpact; most of them are weak, one is broken, and another is still vibrant and powerful. Nale breaks away and leaves, and Dalinar is left considering the importance of his powers and the kind of work that it would enable him to do—specifically that of reforging the Oathpact.

## Rowan's "If Asked" Notes

**Q: What is the Oathpact?**  
A: The Oathpact was an agreement made by the ten Heralds to prevent the Desolations. They bound themselves to it, and when they died, they would go to Braize (Damnation) to be tortured until they broke and the next Desolation began. Dalinar sees it as eight lines of light extending from Nale—most are weak, one is broken (Jezrien, who was killed), and one is still vibrant and powerful (Ishar).

**Q: Why does Nale attack Dalinar?**  
A: Nale believes Dalinar's war is unjust and that he should submit. As a Skybreaker, Nale follows the law, and he seems to believe that the current war violates some higher law. He also questions how Dalinar has access to the surges of Honor, suggesting he thinks it shouldn't be possible.

**Q: What does Dalinar see when he touches Nale?**  
A: When Dalinar touches Nale, he forges a Connection with him. He sees flashes of Nale's past and eight lines of light extending into the distance. These lines are the Oathpact, connecting Nale to the other Heralds. Most are weak, one is broken (Jezrien), and one is still vibrant (Ishar).

**Q: What does it mean to reforge the Oathpact?**  
A: Dalinar realizes that his Bondsmith powers could potentially allow him to reforge the Oathpact—essentially recreate or repair the ancient agreement that bound the Heralds. This would be a momentous act that could have major implications for preventing future Desolations or dealing with Odium.

**Q: Why is one line broken and one vibrant?**  
A: The broken line is Jezrien, who was killed in Oathbringer. The vibrant line is Ishar, who is still active and powerful. The other lines are weak, suggesting the Oathpact is in poor condition overall.

**Q: What is the Urithiru report about?**  
A: The report claims that those at the tower accidentally deployed a device that interferes with both Radiant powers and the Oathgates. They could only deliver the message by hiking out of range. Dalinar and the Mink suspect Taravangian intentionally caused this to isolate them during his betrayal, though they believe there's more to it.

**Q: Why is Szeth protecting Dalinar?**  
A: Szeth is Dalinar's bodyguard and Truthless. He's in disguise at Dalinar's side and grabs him away when Nale appears, showing his loyalty and protective role.

**Q: What does Nale mean about the surges of Honor?**  
A: Nale questions how Dalinar has access to the surges of Honor, saying it shouldn't be possible. This may relate to the fact that Honor is dead, or to some other aspect of how Bondsmith powers work. It's a significant question that suggests there's something unusual about Dalinar's abilities.
