## Quick Summary
Venli is practicing her powers, communicating with the stone and learning of its history while remembering her own. She realizes how much she has lied to herself and feels guilty for all that she has done. She attunes the tones of both Cultivation and Odium in harmony and is able to change the stone.

Venli meets with Leshwi and tells her that the Pursuer has found Kaladin's parents. Leshwi believes they are protected for now by Raboniel, and they discuss Raboniel's complex long-term schemes. Leshwi reminds Venli that the reason they watch is so that they can help ensure that the world will survive Raboniel. Venli goes to see Kaladin's parents and finds Rlain and Lirin in an argument. Dabbid and Rlain have come to report the severity of Kaladin's injuries, but Rlain won't let Lirin go to him unless Lirin promises that he won't turn Kaladin over to the Fused. Lirin stubbornly insists he won't make that promise. Hesina volunteers to go, but Lirin tells her that she lacks the skill to perform surgery if he needs it. Timbre pushes Venli, and Venli speaks up to suggest freeing Lift.

Venli, Rlain, and Dabbid make their way to the cells, and Venli uses her powers to open a hole in the back of Lift's cell. She sends Lift with Rlain and Dabbid after extracting a promise not to speak of the nature of her powers. Venli asks Timbre if she can say the words yet, but Timbre says not yet.

## Rowan's "If Asked" Notes

**Q: How does Venli change the stone?**  
A: Venli attunes the tones of both Cultivation and Odium in harmony and is able to change the stone. This shows her growing ability to work with different types of Investiture and her connection to both Shards. It's similar to how Navani and Raboniel mixed Lights by harmonizing rhythms.

**Q: Why does Venli feel guilty?**  
A: Venli realizes how much she has lied to herself and feels guilty for all that she has done. This is part of her character development—she's coming to terms with her past actions and the harm she's caused, which is necessary for her to progress as a Radiant.

**Q: Why won't Lirin promise not to turn Kaladin over?**  
A: Rlain won't let Lirin go to Kaladin unless Lirin promises that he won't turn Kaladin over to the Fused. However, Lirin stubbornly insists he won't make that promise. This reflects Lirin's pacifist philosophy—he may believe that turning Kaladin over would prevent more violence, even though it would mean Kaladin's capture.

**Q: Why does Venli free Lift?**  
A: Timbre pushes Venli, and Venli speaks up to suggest freeing Lift. This is because Lift is an Edgedancer who can heal, and she may be able to help Kaladin. Venli uses her powers to open a hole in the back of Lift's cell and sends her with Rlain and Dabbid to help Kaladin.

**Q: What does Venli make them promise?**  
A: Venli extracts a promise from Rlain and Dabbid not to speak of the nature of her powers. This is because she's been hiding her Radiant abilities, and if the Fused learn about them, she'll be in danger. She's taking a risk by using her powers to free Lift.

**Q: Why can't Venli say the words yet?**  
A: Venli asks Timbre if she can say the words (her next Radiant ideal) yet, but Timbre says not yet. This suggests that Venli still needs to grow or change before she can progress to the next ideal. Her guilt and realization about lying to herself may be part of this process.

**Q: What are Raboniel's schemes?**  
A: Leshwi and Venli discuss Raboniel's complex long-term schemes. Leshwi reminds Venli that the reason they watch is so that they can help ensure the world will survive Raboniel. This suggests that Raboniel's plans may be dangerous or far-reaching, and that Leshwi and Venli are working to counter them.

**Q: Why is the Pursuer looking for Kaladin's parents?**  
A: Venli tells Leshwi that the Pursuer has found Kaladin's parents. Leshwi believes they are protected for now by Raboniel, but the Pursuer's interest suggests he may be trying to use them to get to Kaladin or to punish him.

**Q: Why is this chapter called "The Games of Men and Singers"?**  
A: The title "The Games of Men and Singers" likely refers to the political maneuvering and conflicts between humans and singers, as well as the "games" being played by various factions (Raboniel, Leshwi, Venli, etc.). It may also refer to the argument between Lirin and Rlain, which represents the conflict between different approaches to dealing with the occupation.
